\title{Supporting Information: Technical Details, Ablations, and Extended Validation\\ for Deployment-Oriented Cooperative Yaw Control}
\maketitle

This Supporting Information provides the full technical audit trail for the six core claims of the main paper:
(1)~the calibrated gray-box wake model is a trustworthy control-oriented surrogate;
(2)~the DRL policy recovers SLSQP-grade static cooperative-yaw performance;
(3)~the DRL policy does not depend on access to the SLSQP oracle;
(4)~the dynamic-wind degradation is linked to wake-propagation delay and partial observability;
(5)~DRL-Deploy---the policy plus supervisory wrapper---is the evaluated controller;
(6)~AEP, safety, and load-proxy results support deployment potential but require field and aeroelastic validation.

All dynamic comparisons use \textbf{percentage farm-power gain} and \textbf{per-turbine yaw travel} under matched trajectories. Training rewards and regret-reward units are not used as deployment metrics.

\section{Gray-Box Wake-Model Calibration and Cross-Validation}\label{sec:wake}

\subsection{CFD reference datasets and calibration}

Three CFD reference datasets were used for joint parameter calibration:
\begin{itemize}
\item Three-turbine inline layout (Supplementary Fig.~S1a): wake-velocity profiles at $d_0$ spacing;
\item 35-turbine large farm at $240^\circ$ inflow (Supplementary Fig.~S1b): farm-scale wake accumulation;
\item Two-turbine yaw-sweep (Supplementary Fig.~S1c): yaw-induced deflection at $\gamma{\in}[0^\circ,50^\circ]$.
\end{itemize}

The Bastankhah--Port\'e-Agel Gaussian wake model was calibrated via Levenberg--Marquardt least-squares optimisation over all three datasets jointly. The free parameters are the wake-spreading coefficients ($\alpha^\star$, $\beta^\star$), the superposition mixing factor ($\alpha$), and the ambient turbulence intensity ($I$). Table~S3 reports the calibrated values.

\subsection{Horns Rev~I validation}

The calibrated model is validated against the Horns Rev~I LES benchmark~(Barthelmie et~al., 2009) using the V80 turbine power curve (Supplementary Fig.~S2). Normalised farm power as a function of wind direction is compared against LES measurements, confirming that the model captures the direction-dependent wake-loss structure without site-specific retuning.

\subsection{FLORIS cross-validation}

Figure~S3 compares the calibrated gray-box model against FLORIS (GCH wake model, NREL~5MW turbine) on the $3{\times}3$ layout for a direction sweep ($\gamma{=}0^\circ$) and a two-turbine yaw sweep ($\gamma{\in}[0^\circ,50^\circ]$). The two models agree on the sign, rank ordering, and location of peak cooperative-yaw gains, supporting the use of the gray-box model as a DRL training surrogate.

\section{PPO Training and Implementation Details}\label{sec:ppo_impl}

\subsection{Observation and action spaces}

The observation vector for history length $J$ is the flattened concatenation of $J$ observation rows. Each row contains: per-turbine yaw angles ($N$), per-turbine inflow speeds ($N$), wind-direction cosine and sine, wind speed ($3$), downstream-lock mask ($N$), and---with position encoding enabled---normalised $(x,y)$ turbine coordinates ($2N$). For $N{=}9$ turbines with position encoding, one row has $3N{+}3{+}2N{=}48$ dimensions. The total observation dimension is $J \times 48$ (Table~S4). The action is an incremental yaw command $\Delta\gamma_i \in [-\mathrm{AB}, +\mathrm{AB}]$ per turbine, accumulated and clipped to $[-50^\circ, +50^\circ]$.

\subsection{JAX/NNX on-device implementation}

The training loop is fully on-device: the wake model, the PPO rollout (128 parallel environments), and the GAE+PPO update are all expressed in JAX and JIT-compiled. This achieves ${\sim}95{,}000$\,FPS on an NVIDIA RTX~4090 (Supplementary Fig.~S4). The JAX environment produces numerically equivalent results to the original NumPy gym on a $19$-point $(\phi,v)$ grid (Supplementary Fig.~S5), confirming that the acceleration does not alter the physical evaluation model.

\subsection{Action-bound sensitivity}

Expanding the action bounds from ${\pm}5^\circ$ to ${\pm}10^\circ$ per step allows the policy to accumulate yaw more rapidly during the settling phase of each condition. Under the focused sampling mixture and $6{\times}10^{7}$-step budget, the ${\pm}10^\circ$ configuration yields an aligned-cube gain of $+5.61\%$ (vs.\ $+4.89\%$ at ${\pm}5^\circ$, Table~S6, Balanced), at the cost of increased dynamic-wind yaw travel (from $52^\circ$ to $210^\circ$/turb).

\subsection{Downstream-lock ablation}

The downstream-turbine locking mechanism prevents turbines that are not significantly waking any downstream neighbour from participating in cooperative yaw. Re-training the \textbf{baseline PPO configuration} (Config-A settings: $J{=}1$, uniform sampling, marginal reward, ${\pm}5^\circ$, $3{\times}10^{7}$ steps, 3 seeds) with the lock disabled degrades the mean gain from $+2.42\%$ to $-0.46\%$ (Supplementary Fig.~S6). Under this baseline configuration, locking is structurally necessary for stable cooperative-yaw learning. The lock mechanism is retained in all subsequent configurations including Config-E.

Note: the main text (Section~3.2) correctly attributes this ablation to the baseline PPO configuration, not to Config-E. The lock was not separately ablated under the full Config-E settings because the Config-A result established its necessity and computational resources were prioritised for the deployment-relevant experiments.

\subsection{KL early-stopping}

KL-targeted early stopping functions as a safety valve rather than a primary regularisation mechanism. Table~S7 reports the final training metrics for two Config-D variants (3 seeds, $3{\times}10^{7}$ steps, TARGET\_KL${=}0.015$ enabled vs.\ disabled via a high threshold). Cosine learning-rate decay and AdamW weight decay are the dominant contributors to Config-D's improved training stability.

\subsection{Reward-penalty ablation}

Adding yaw-magnitude and yaw-rate penalties ($\lambda_\mathrm{mag}{=}\lambda_\mathrm{rate}{=}8{\times}10^{-5}$) to the training reward depresses the final-$10$-iteration return from $+19.79$ to $+15.78$ ($-20\%$ relative) and lowers the best-seed peak from $+28.1$ to $+22.3$ (Supplementary Fig.~S7). This result informed the decision to introduce rate penalties only through dynamic-wind training ($\lambda_\mathrm{rate}$, Section~S6) rather than via static-training reward shaping.

\section{Reward and Algorithmic Baselines}\label{sec:baselines}

\subsection{Oracle-free marginal-reward ablation}

The SLSQP-regret reward uses a precomputed SLSQP lookup table ($91{\times}11$ grid) to provide a headroom signal during training. To test whether the DRL policy's cooperative-yaw capability depends on this signal, we trained a Config-E variant with a marginal reward (power gain relative to zero-yaw baseline, no SLSQP reference). Table~S8 reports the full seed-wise comparison (3 seeds, $6{\times}10^{7}$ steps, Config-E settings).

\begin{table}[h]

\label{tab:reward_abl}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Reward & AC gain [\%] & Dynamic gain/step [\%] & Travel/turb [$^\circ$] \\
\midrule
SLSQP-regret & $+4.48\,\pm\,6.17$ & $-0.158$ & $210$ \\

The marginal-reward policy reaches $92.2\%$ of the SLSQP-regret aligned-cube gain, confirming that the DRL policy does not depend on the SLSQP oracle for its cooperative-yaw capability. The SLSQP-regret formulation acts as a sample-efficiency accelerator---it raises the static peak---while the oracle-free policy exhibits lower dynamic-wind travel, consistent with a more conservative action profile. These data are reported as 3-seed means from the reward-ablation experiment; full seed-wise data are available in \texttt{results/reward\_ablation.json}.

\subsection{Behavioral Cloning baseline}

A Behavioral Cloning (BC) baseline was trained via supervised regression to predict SLSQP-optimal yaw vectors from wind conditions, using a dataset of $1{,}000$ SLSQP solutions sampled from the training distribution. The BC policy uses the same $[128,128]$ MLP architecture as the PPO actor and was trained for $100$ epochs with MSE loss.

Evaluated on $100$ uniformly sampled wind conditions, the BC policy achieves a marginal mean gain of $-20.20\%$ and an aligned-cube gain of $-31.50\%$ ($n{=}7$ conditions)---substantially worse than the zero-yaw baseline. The result suggests that direct supervised regression to SLSQP yaw vectors is insufficient under the present dataset size and architecture, likely because the SLSQP yaw mapping contains discontinuities near regime boundaries. The DRL policy, by contrast, learns through interaction and produces smoother yaw trajectories. The BC result is reported as an auxiliary negative baseline; it is not a central claim of the paper.

\subsection{SAC baseline}

A JAX/NNX-native Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent was implemented on the same on-device stack. The SAC agent uses twin Q-networks with $[128,128]$ MLPs (identical to PPO), a Gaussian policy with tanh squashing, automatic entropy tuning, and a replay buffer of $5{\times}10^{5}$ transitions. Training used $N_\mathrm{envs}{=}4$ parallel environments (vs.\ PPO's $128$; the lower count is a consequence of JIT compilation constraints for SAC's multi-gradient update structure) and $N_\mathrm{steps}{=}512$ steps per rollout. Five seeds were trained for $3{\times}10^{7}$ environment steps each under Config-E settings.

At the matched $3{\times}10^{7}$-step budget, SAC achieves a five-seed-mean final episode return of $+185.2\,\pm\,75.3$ (regret-reward units), compared to PPO Config-E's $+334.3\,\pm\,217.0$. SAC reaches $55.4\%$ of PPO's mean reward with substantially lower inter-seed variance. The throughput gap is $13{\times}$ (${\sim}7{,}100$\,FPS for SAC vs.\ ${\sim}95{,}000$\,FPS for PPO). We attribute SAC's underperformance to off-policy sample inefficiency when the reward signal is concentrated in a narrow wind regime (${\sim}9\%$ of conditions), and to the squashed-Gaussian action parameterisation penalising large yaw increments.

PPO is retained as the primary algorithm because it offers the best empirical performance--throughput compromise under the present implementation. SAC is not claimed to be unsuitable for wind-farm control in general; a pure-JAX refactoring of the gradient computation is expected to narrow the throughput gap.

\subsection{Interpretation}

Raw static DRL and the SLSQP lookup table occupy opposite extremes of the power--travel trade-off. Raw DRL has low travel but negative mean gain; the unlimited lookup has high gain but $42.55^\circ\!/\!\mathrm{s}$ peak rate---far beyond the $0.3$--$0.5^\circ\!/\!\mathrm{s}$ capability of utility-scale yaw drives. The DRL-Deploy results in Table~S10 (Protocol~B) represent the final deployment-oriented evaluation.

\section{Extended Observation Horizon and Wake-Delay Hypothesis}\label{sec:j_extended}

\subsection{Training setup}

To test the wake-propagation-delay hypothesis---that a short observation window cannot perceive the causal effect of yaw actions on downstream turbines, causing spurious wind--action correlations---we trained Config-E policies with observation histories $J{=}3,8,15$ under dynamic AR(1) wind. All policies use $\lambda_\mathrm{rate}{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$, $6{\times}10^{7}$ training steps, and 5 seeds. The observation dimensions are given in Table~S4. $J{=}15$ ($150$\,s at $T{=}10$\,s) spans the ${\sim}90$--$110$\,s wake-propagation delay at $7d_0$ spacing and $8$--$10$\,m/s inflow.

\subsection{Results}

Table~S11 reports the 5-seed dynamic-wind results under both protocols. Extending the observation window to $J{=}15$ materially improves dynamic performance relative to $J{=}3$ (Proto~B: $-0.534\%$ vs.\ $-1.210\%$), while $J{=}8$ is inconclusive and does not consistently improve over the short-window baseline (the J=8 Proto~A result is marginally worse than J=3). The improvement is therefore concentrated at the longest observation horizon, consistent with the hypothesis that the observational window must substantially exceed the wake-propagation delay to provide useful temporal context.

\section{DRL-Deploy Wrapper Sensitivity}\label{sec:deploy_sens}

\subsection{Wrapper definition}

The DRL-Deploy controller is defined in Algorithm~S1. The supervisory wrapper applies four sequential layers to the raw DRL policy output: (i)~sector gate with hysteresis, (ii)~yaw-command deadband, (iii)~per-step rate limit, and (iv)~zero-yaw fallback. The gate and fallback use only real-time wind measurements available to any SCADA system.

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{Algorithm S1:} DRL-Deploy control cycle (one step, $T{=}10$\,s).\\
\textbf{Input:} wind direction $\phi_t$, wind speed $v_t$, previous yaw $\gamma_{t-1}$, gate state $g_{t-1} \in \{0,1\}$\\
\textbf{Output:} yaw command $\gamma_t$, gate state $g_t$\\
1.\ \ $d_\phi \leftarrow \min(|\phi_t - 270^\circ|, 360^\circ - |\phi_t - 270^\circ|)$\\
2.\ \ $\theta \leftarrow 20^\circ$ if $g_{t-1}{=}1$ else $15^\circ$ \hfill // hysteresis\\
3.\ \ $g_t \leftarrow (d_\phi < \theta) \land (v_t < 11.4\,\mathrm{m/s})$\\
4.\ \ if $g_t = 0$: output $\gamma_t \leftarrow 0^\circ$; return \hfill // fallback\\
5.\ \ $\Delta\hat{\gamma} \leftarrow \pi_\mathrm{DRL}(\text{obs}_t)$ \hfill // PPO forward pass\\
6.\ \ if $\max_i|\Delta\hat{\gamma}_i| < 2^\circ$: $\Delta\hat{\gamma} \leftarrow 0$ \hfill // deadband\\
7.\ \ $\Delta\gamma \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}(\Delta\hat{\gamma},\ -3^\circ,\ +3^\circ)$ \hfill // rate limit\\
8.\ \ $\gamma_t \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}(\gamma_{t-1} + \Delta\gamma,\ -50^\circ,\ +50^\circ)$\\
9.\ \ output $\gamma_t$, $g_t$
\end{quote}

Table~S14 reports sensitivity to the gate entry band ($10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, $20^\circ$) with hysteresis exit at entry${+}5^\circ$. \subsection{Rate-limit verification}

The per-step rate limit of $3^\circ$ corresponds to $0.3^\circ\!/\!\mathrm{s}$ at $T{=}10$\,s. This is consistent with the $0.3$--$0.5^\circ\!/\!\mathrm{s}$ yaw-rate capability of multi-MW utility-scale turbines. The DRL-Deploy peak yaw rates in Table~S10 ($1.86$--$2.64^\circ\!/\!\mathrm{s}$ reported as per-trajectory maximum rate, not per-step average) reflect transient per-step maxima within the $3^\circ$/step bound, not sustained rates.

\section{Horns Rev AEP Replay Details}\label{sec:aep_details}

\subsection{Wind-climate model}

The Horns Rev~I wind climatology is modelled as a Weibull wind-speed distribution ($k{=}2.1$, $A{=}10.5$\,m/s at $70$\,m hub height) and a von Mises directional distribution ($\mu{=}270^\circ$, $\kappa{=}2.0$), based on published measurements~(Barthelmie et~al., 2009; Hansen et~al., 2012). The baseline annual energy production is $500\,\mathrm{MW} \times 0.45$ capacity factor $\times 8{,}760\,\mathrm{h} = 1{,}971{,}000$\,MWh/yr. The aligned-cube occurrence probability (i.i.d.\ sampling) is $9.1\%$, and the near-aligned probability ($5^\circ{<}|\Delta\phi|{\le}15^\circ$) is $17.6\%$.

\subsection{Monte Carlo sampling and annualisation}

$n{=}5{,}000$ wind conditions are sampled i.i.d.\ from the joint distribution. Each condition is evaluated with $10$-step settling (the DRL policy accumulates yaw through repeated incremental actions over the settling phase; the final-step power is used for the gain calculation). Annual yaw travel is annualised as $\mathrm{travel}_\mathrm{annual} = \bar{t}_\mathrm{cond} \times 8{,}760 \times 3{,}600 / (T \times N_\mathrm{settle}) / 10^6$ [Mdeg/yr], where $\bar{t}_\mathrm{cond}$ is the mean per-condition travel.

\subsection{Key findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Raw DRL is inherently unsafe}: $728$ unsafe commands in clean conditions, $45.5^\circ$ max yaw.
\item \textbf{DRL-Deploy wrapper provides substantial safety improvement}: unsafe commands reduced by $61\%$ (Static) to $100\%$ (J=15) relative to Raw DRL.
\item \textbf{Yaw-angle sensor bias is the most critical fault mode}: $\pm5^\circ$ bias produces $1{,}242$--$1{,}350$ unsafe commands for Static Deploy, with max yaw reaching $55^\circ$ (near the $\pm50^\circ$ physical limit). Yaw-encoder redundancy is recommended for field deployments.
\item \textbf{Gate provides automatic high-wind protection}: $100\%$ fallback activation at $v{\geq}17$\,m/s.
\item \textbf{Sudden wind shifts are handled gracefully}: DRL-Deploy returns to zero yaw within the gate exit band; Raw DRL exhibits catastrophic gain collapse ($+40.0\%{\to}{-}21.7\%$) on a $20^\circ$ shift.
\item \textbf{Measurement dropout ($10\%$) induces negligible degradation}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Load Proxy and Yaw-Activity Analysis}\label{sec:load}

\section{Scalability and Layout Generalization}\label{sec:scale}

\subsection{$5{\times}5$ layout}

Config-E policies were trained on a $5{\times}5$ regular grid ($25$ turbines, $7d_0$ spacing, $3$ seeds, $6{\times}10^{7}$ steps) with position encoding enabled. The $5{\times}5$ aligned-cube gain is $+4.70\%$ ($n{=}51$ conditions, 3-seed mean), transferring $95.7\%$ of the $3{\times}3$ headline ($+4.91\%$). Without position encoding, the $5{\times}5$ gain was $+0.98\%$, confirming that position encoding is essential for larger layouts where the policy must distinguish between turbines at different spatial coordinates.

\subsection{Irregular layout transfer}

A Config-E policy trained on the regular $3{\times}3$ grid was evaluated zero-shot on an irregular $9$-turbine layout. The zero-shot aligned-cube gain is $-1.41\%$ (negative, indicating the policy actively reduces farm power). Retraining the same Config-E architecture on the irregular layout from scratch recovers a positive aligned-cube gain of $+1.39\%$ (3 seeds, $6{\times}10^{7}$ steps). A staggered $3{\times}3$ layout (partial intermediate regularity) achieves $+1.61\%$ zero-shot transfer.

These results indicate that the DRL framework supports site-specific retraining but does not generalise zero-shot to arbitrarily irregular layouts. Position encoding provides partial transfer capability, but for deployment on a new layout, retraining with the target turbine coordinates is recommended.
